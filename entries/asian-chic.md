# Asian Chic (Contemporary Asian Heritage Registers)

"Asian chic" is a loose umbrella, not a style: it usually means
heritage forms from a specific Asian tradition made modern with
restrained, minimal styling. The entry's main job is disambiguation.
Resolve the brief to **one named tradition** and build from that
tradition's own materials, colours and type. A mix of cultures, or
generic "Oriental" shorthand, is the most common failure.

## You know it when you see

- Restrained surfaces (white, natural wood, paper, stone) carrying one
  or two heritage details: a lacquer red, an ink-wash image, a lattice
  pattern, a woven texture
- Calm, asymmetric layouts with deliberate empty space
- Native-script typography used as real typography, not as decoration
- Natural materials: bamboo, rattan, linen, ceramic, lacquer, paper
- The failures: "chopstick" or faux-brush "wonton" fonts for Latin text;
  a red-and-gold-dragon costume; mixing Japanese, Chinese and Korean
  motifs into one look; [[ornament-traditions]]'s chinoiserie (a European
  fantasy of Asia) presented as the real thing

## Dials

- **tradition** (pick one, and ask the designer if the brief doesn't
  say):
  - *Japanese modern*: ma (negative space), wabi-sabi imperfection,
    mingei craft, sumi ink, indigo and natural tones, asymmetry; see
    Japandi in [[soft-aesthetics]]
  - *New Chinese style* (xin zhongshi) and *guochao*: lacquer red,
    jade/celadon, ink-wash landscapes, lattice screens; guochao is the
    louder, youthful national-trend version with bold colour and
    reworked heritage motifs
  - *Korean modern*: hanok calm, hanji paper light, bojagi patchwork,
    obangsaek (five-colour) accents used sparingly
  - *Southeast Asian / tropical modern*: rattan and teak, batik or ikat
    textiles, open airy layouts, lush green imagery
- **heritage dose**: one detail on a modern base (chic) vs full
  decorative immersion (themed). Chic means a low dose
- **script**: use real native-script typefaces where text appears; pair
  with a neutral Latin face rather than an "Asian-looking" Latin font
- **palette**: drawn from the tradition's materials (ink, paper, lacquer,
  celadon, indigo, rattan), not a generic red-gold
- **space & asymmetry**: generous negative space, off-centre balance
  ([[visual-composition]])

## Techniques

- **Ask, then research**: confirm the tradition and audience; source
  motifs from museum collections and designers working in that tradition.
- **Typography**: native-script fonts with proper language tagging
  (`lang` attributes for correct CJK glyph forms); a quiet Latin sans or
  serif beside them.
- **Material textures**: paper, wood and woven textures as subtle
  backgrounds ([[material-texture]]); lattice or pattern as thin SVG
  borders.
- **Imagery**: ink-wash or photography in the tradition's own idiom, with
  muted grading ([[image-treatment]]).

## Starting points

- Asia Design Prize trend reports (recurring themes: restrained white
  surfaces, minimal structure, differences in light and material)
- Museum collections for motif reference by tradition
